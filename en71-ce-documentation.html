01 / Test evidence

What EN 71-1, EN 71-2 and EN 71-3 cover

EN 71-1

Mechanical and physical properties. Review applicable hazards such as small parts, balls, edges, moving components and magnets against the intended age group and actual set.

EN 71-2

Flammability. Check which requirements apply to the materials and accessories in the proposed toy, and how any exclusions are justified.

EN 71-3

Migration of certain elements. Match the tested accessible materials, colours, inks and coatings to the component list. Piece count does not determine the material sampling groups.

Check editions, amendments and restrictions against the EU harmonised standards references and the consolidated standards decision. Additional chemical, electrical or other requirements depend on the actual product and destination.

02 / Magnetic components

Review the magnet evidence for the actual construction

EN 71-1 includes requirements for magnets and magnetic components. Check the identified components, small-parts findings and magnetic flux index where applicable, including the findings after the required use-and-abuse sequence.

For a project review, we compare the magnet specification, shell, joining method and accessories with the tested sample records. A change in any of these can affect the evidence needed. A description of sealing or rivets alone cannot establish the result.

Official background: CASP2022 toys with magnets report, section 1.2.2. The safety testing guide gives a wider report-reading checklist.

03 / The market file

CE marking, the declaration and technical documentation

CE marking expresses the manufacturer's responsibility for meeting applicable EU requirements. A sample test report supports that work. Before market placement, the responsible manufacturer must complete the safety assessment and appropriate conformity assessment, assemble technical documentation and sign the declaration of conformity.

Test evidence
Identifies the sample, methods, scope and findings, with supporting photographs and annexes.
Technical documentation
Connects design and manufacturing information, materials and components, the safety assessment, supporting evidence and production consistency records.
Declaration of Conformity (DoC)
Identifies the responsible manufacturer, product and applicable requirements. For toys under Directive 2009/48/EC, the formal title is EC Declaration of Conformity; it is also commonly called an EU DoC.
Final product information
Aligns the marking, manufacturer and importer details, product and batch identification, instructions, warnings and required languages with the destination.

The assessment route depends on the requirements covered and the standards applied. A notified body is involved where the applicable route requires it. Source: European Commission: placing toys on the EU market.

04 / Destination route

UKCA, Great Britain and Northern Ireland

Great Britain: England, Scotland and Wales

Current guidance accepts CE alongside UKCA. A CE route uses an EU DoC; a UKCA route uses a UK DoC. Confirm the route, relevant evidence, English safety information and UK importer details.

A second complete test package is not automatically required. Review any actual differences. See GOV.UK: GB toy safety guidance and, for a UKCA route, GB designated standards.

Northern Ireland: review separately

UKCA alone is insufficient. CE applies; CE plus UKNI is required when a UK body performs mandatory third-party assessment. CE plus UKNI does not give access to the EU market.

Confirm the responsible economic operator and destination file under the separate NI guidance.

05 / Plan by market placement

EU Toy Safety Regulation: the transition

Regulation (EU) 2025/2509 entered into force on 1 January 2026. Its main product requirements, including the digital product passport framework, apply from 1 August 2030. Articles 28–44 and 49–55 apply from 1 January 2026.

Toys lawfully placed on the market under Directive 2009/48/EC before 1 August 2030 may continue to be made available under the transition. Plan against the first placing-on-the-market date; a manufacturing or test date alone does not establish eligibility.

This transition does not establish coverage of an existing sample report for a different set. We review current requirements and future document needs for the selected project. Source: Regulation (EU) 2025/2509, Articles 56–59. GB and NI implications need their own review.

06 / Agree who is responsible

What brands, manufacturers and importers prepare

Identify the legal roles before agreeing the packaging and document file. A brand placing a product under its own name or trademark can take on manufacturer duties; confirm that role for the proposed supply arrangement.

Responsible manufacturer / own-brand business

Own the product definition, safety and conformity assessment, technical file, signed declaration, production consistency and change control. Keep the required records available.

Importer

Verify the manufacturer's assessment and documents, required marking, traceability, instructions and warnings. Add the required importer identification, retain the declaration and ensure authorities can obtain technical documentation. Manage storage, transport and corrective action duties.

The EU and GB roles and labelling rules must be checked separately. See Directive 2009/48/EC, Articles 4, 6, 8 and Annex IV and GOV.UK GB guidance, sections 5–7.

07 / Our project review

How we check the selected set

We have EN 71 reports for selected samples; coverage of the current sets and target-market documentation is confirmed per project.

  1. Fix the SKU and configuration.

    Record the component list, quantities, version, colours, accessories, intended age group and sample photographs.

  2. Map the available evidence.

    Compare the sample identity, tested materials, magnet construction, scope and exclusions with that configuration. Record gaps explicitly.

  3. Link the production batch.

    Check the specification revision, production reference and changes in materials, components or joining methods. Decide what needs further assessment.

  4. Check the destination and packaging.

    Review the chosen market route, responsible parties, declaration, technical file, product identification, marking, warnings, instructions and artwork revision.

  5. Agree the open actions.

    Record evidence that can be used, questions still open, any additional testing or document work, and who will resolve each item before market placement.

For configuration identity only: D01-C has 102 pieces, D10-C 219, A01-T 119, A03-T 73 and C01-C 99. Counts include the listed accessories; manuals are listed separately. These counts do not establish report coverage.

09 / Buyer questions

EN 71 / CE documentation FAQ

What test-document coverage can you confirm for the current sets?

We have EN 71 reports for selected samples; coverage of the current sets and target-market documentation is confirmed per project.

Is an EN 71 report the same as CE documentation?

An EN 71 report records the scope examined for an identified sample. The CE file also needs the applicable safety assessment, conformity assessment, technical documentation, signed declaration and marking.

Do magnetic tiles need a separate magnet review?

Yes. Identify the magnet and magnetic component, applicable EN 71-1 requirements, small-parts findings and magnetic flux index where relevant. Match the evidence to the actual construction and the required use-and-abuse sequence.

Does Great Britain require UKCA instead of CE?

Current GB guidance accepts the CE route alongside UKCA. Check the chosen route, its declaration and GB duties. Northern Ireland requires a separate review; UKCA alone is insufficient there.

When do the EU Toy Safety Regulation requirements apply?

Regulation (EU) 2025/2509 entered into force on 1 January 2026. Its main product requirements apply from 1 August 2030; Articles 28–44 and 49–55 apply earlier. Use the first placing-on-the-market date when reviewing the transition.
