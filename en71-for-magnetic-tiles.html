EN 71 for magnetic tiles: what importers should check

Reviewing EN 71 for magnetic tiles means checking which safety requirements apply to the complete set and whether the evidence matches the version you plan to import. Before an EU launch, importers need to connect product identity, assessment scope, documentation and legal responsibilities. Use the six checks below to record a decision and identify what must be resolved before market placement.

EN 71 for magnetic tiles: start with the legal framework

For EU projects, separate three questions: which legal requirements apply, which standards support the assessment, and whether the evidence describes the selected product. EN 71 is a series of toy safety standards. Its name on a document does not settle the other two questions.

Under the current directive framework, a manufacturer completes the safety and conformity assessments, prepares technical documentation and signs the EC Declaration of Conformity. CE marking expresses the manufacturer's responsibility. Importers have their own verification duties. The European Commission's market-placement guidance explains these roles and the available assessment routes.

Harmonised standards can support a presumption of conformity for the requirements they cover. Check the actual references, restrictions and relevant dates in the Commission's toy standards register. A more recent publication date on a standard or report does not, by itself, resolve its status under EU law.

Step 1: record the destination and market-placement date

Write down the EU destination, planned first market placement and business responsible for that decision. Keep this beside the product brief. It prevents a discussion about one market's documents from quietly becoming an approval for another destination.

Regulation (EU) 2025/2509 entered into force on 1 January 2026. Its main product requirements, including the digital product passport framework, apply from 1 August 2030; Articles 28–44 and 49–55 apply earlier. The regulation's Articles 56–59 set out the transition and application dates.

For planning, distinguish an order date, manufacturing date and first market-placement date. Record which framework your reviewer is using and schedule a fresh check if the launch moves. The transition should be a dated planning entry, rather than a reason to assume that an existing file covers a future launch.

Step 2: define the complete set before reviewing evidence

Create a reference containing the SKU, configuration revision, component quantities, colours, accessories, intended age group and photographs. Include the proposed box and instructions. Resolve differences between the commercial SKU and any laboratory sample code in a written cross-reference.

A plain-tile configuration and a configuration containing a car or ball-run accessories need separate scope checks. The D01 magnetic tile product page and C01-C track set product page are starting points for identifying configurations. A product page cannot establish the connection to a laboratory sample.

Keep one row for every difference you find. A matching photograph or total piece count helps identify the product, but leaves materials, internal construction and later changes to be resolved. Record the person who will confirm each connection.

Step 3: check the applicable EN 71 parts and editions

Use a scope table with columns for the standard, edition, relevant component, supporting evidence and unresolved question. The consolidated toy standards decision identifies the published references, including:

  • EN 71-1: mechanical and physical properties; locate the magnet, small-parts and other relevant mechanical findings.
  • EN 71-2: flammability; establish which requirements concern the selected materials and accessories.
  • EN 71-3: migration of certain elements; connect the examined materials and colours to the component list.

Let the product's safety assessment determine the remaining scope. Electrical functions or other added features may introduce further requirements. Keep a documented reason for exclusions. Three familiar standard names should lead to a component-level review, rather than end it.

Step 4: follow the magnet evidence through the construction

For each magnetic component, locate the sample identification, relevant small-parts assessment, magnetic flux index where applicable, and findings after the specified use-and-abuse sequence. The Commission's CASP2022 toys with magnets report, section 1.2.2, explains the relationship between detachable magnetic components, size and magnetic flux index.

Turn that framework into review questions: Which tile shape or accessory does this result describe? Which construction revision was examined? Where are the observations after the relevant mechanical tests? If several components are grouped, what supports that grouping? Keep the answers next to the configuration reference.

Attraction during play, an intact-looking shell and a joining-process description answer different questions from a laboratory assessment. Use them to identify the sample and raise questions. Leave missing findings open until the responsible reviewer resolves them. The safety testing guide provides broader evidence-reading fields.

Step 5: connect the evidence to the manufacturer's file

Check that the declaration identifies the responsible manufacturer and product, and that its legislation and standards references agree with the assessment. Establish how the technical documentation can be made available to authorities. A laboratory report is supporting evidence within that file; the Commission's technical documentation guide explains the file's wider contents.

For importers, Directive 2009/48/EC, Article 6, includes retaining a declaration copy for ten years after market placement and ensuring access to technical documentation. Article 8 addresses own-name or trademark arrangements that bring manufacturer duties.

Keep role decisions visible before artwork approval. Compare the required manufacturer and importer identification, product or batch reference, marking, instructions and warning languages across the physical sample and final artwork. If the brand, legal entity or product reference differs, hold the relevant approval until the relationship is explained.

Step 6: make an importer review decision

Use this decision checklist at the end of the review. Record an evidence location and named reviewer for every answer:

  • Is the destination and intended market-placement date recorded?
  • Does the product reference identify all components and revisions?
  • Are relevant standard editions, restrictions and exclusions accounted for?
  • Can the magnetic findings be traced to the actual construction?
  • Are the declaration, technical-file access and legal roles settled?
  • Do artwork, instructions and traceability match the reviewed product?
  • Are changes and remaining actions assigned a deadline?

Continue review when the evidence is traceable and the next actions are clear. Hold approval when identity or scope is unresolved. Escalate contradictory findings to the responsible manufacturer and reviewer. Completing a checklist is an internal review milestone; market placement still depends on fulfilling the applicable obligations.

Common pitfalls that leave the decision unfinished

Stopping at the cover page. Review the findings, sample photographs and annexes together. Treating an older date as the whole answer. Examine product changes and applicable requirements before deciding whether evidence remains useful. Approving artwork first. Resolve legal identities and warning languages while revisions are still being prepared.

Losing the production connection. Keep the specification revision, batch reference and change record linked to the assessment. If a material, magnet, joining method or accessory changes, send that difference back for review. Use the first-order safety document checklist to organise those records and deadlines.

Importer questions

Is an EN 71 report enough to place magnetic tiles on the EU market?

A report supports the assessment of an identified sample. The responsible manufacturer also needs the applicable safety and conformity assessments, technical documentation, declaration and marking. The importer must complete its own checks. Review the complete product file before making the market-placement decision.

Does every order or batch need new testing?

A new order reference alone does not decide the testing scope. Review the product revision, construction, materials, applicable requirements and production connection. Record the responsible reviewer’s decision about whether further assessment or testing is needed.

What if the sample code differs from the SKU on my order?

Keep the sample code, commercial SKU and configuration revision together in a written cross-reference. Match the component list and photographs, then resolve remaining differences. A similar product name or matching piece count leaves the connection unfinished.

Can an importer also have manufacturer duties?

Yes. Under the directive framework, an importer or distributor placing a toy under its own name or trademark can have manufacturer duties. Confirm the legal role for the proposed arrangement before approving the declaration, marking and packaging.

Prepare your importer review brief

List the selected SKU, configuration revision, EU destination, planned market-placement date and unresolved document questions. Use the existing inquiry form to discuss the project. Keep the evidence and role decisions together as the product definition develops.
