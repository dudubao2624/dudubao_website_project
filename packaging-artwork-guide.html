Private label magnetic tiles: packaging & artwork guide

A useful packaging brief connects your brand artwork to a specific magnetic tile set. Before laying out the box, choose the SKU, component mix, intended market and packaging direction. That gives your designer and our project team a shared starting point for the sample and artwork review.

Discuss your projectExplore OEM options

1 · Define the product behind the box

List the model and version, quantity per SKU and colorway, and whether the proposal uses an existing set configuration or a changed component mix. Identify the packaging format you want to discuss and any instructions or inserts. Our existing OEM examples include retail boxes, a MINI tin with a booklet, packaging artwork and direct tile printing. These are useful references when explaining your direction.

Give your designer the agreed component list and a representative build image. Keep product photos and box-content illustrations tied to that list. If an image combines pieces from different sets, identify the additional pieces before it is considered for packaging. This is particularly relevant when presenting a complete ball run alongside a separate bell expansion pack.

2 · Prepare your logo and brand files

Prepare an editable vector logo, for example an AI file or vector PDF, plus a preview showing how it should look. Confirm the export requirements for your project before handover. Keep any text, symbols and brand colors consistent across the box, booklet and other printed items.

A small screenshot can explain a concept, but your designer should also supply the artwork needed to reproduce the final design. List fonts, linked images and any special print effects you want reviewed. Name one person on your side to consolidate comments, and give every file a clear SKU, revision and date so an old preview cannot be confused with the approved artwork.

3 · Confirm the dieline and dimensions before layout

We supply the dieline and dimensions for your selected packaging, so your designer can work on the exact template before starting final layout. Packaging dielines are model-specific, so use a project-confirmed version. Check any earlier file or reference box dimension with us before adapting a design.

Have your designer identify folds, cuts, front and back panels, and the intended locations of logos, barcodes and text on the confirmed template. Ask which areas need to stay clear for assembly or handling. Agree the template and artwork revision together before requesting a packaging sample. Exact measurements and print settings belong in the project files once confirmed.

4 · Specify colors, copy and market information

Provide your brand color references and indicate which colors apply to printed artwork and which apply to product components. Ask to review appearance on the agreed sample, including the relationship between the box, instructions and product. Record any color comments against a particular file and sample version, rather than approving an unnamed image in a message thread.

Supply the intended languages, product name, component count, barcode information and any market-specific text for review. Share your destination and documentation requirements with the project team. Third-party test reports available on request. Confirm the wording and documentation relevant to the selected product before approving the artwork; a packaging example alone does not establish the requirements for your market.

5 · Keep a clear sample approval record

Review the product configuration, packaging layout, logo placement, copy, colors, instructions and included accessories together. Mark each item approved or needing revision, with a photo or file reference. When a change affects the component mix or box layout, ask whether a revised sample or further review is needed. Confirm the final artwork, sample and order specification before proceeding to production arrangements.

For a quotation, send the SKU and color quantities, market, packaging brief, logo preview and sample needs. Private-label orders start at 500 sets per SKU; special packaging or dedicated accessories may require a higher MOQ. Use the MOQ and sample checklist to plan the review, or see the existing OEM packaging examples for visual references.
