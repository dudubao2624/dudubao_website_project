01 · Injection molding

Injection molding is one of the production processes documented in the factory material. For your project, start with the selected shape and component list. If you need a different structure or a new shape, identify it separately from a color change. New structures and tooling need their own feasibility, testing, commercial and IP review. Confirm the agreed product specification before treating a proposed modification as part of the order.

02 · Magnet insertion and ultrasonic welding

The assembly image shows magnetic tiles at a magnet insertion and ultrasonic welding station. When reviewing a sample, record the exact model and component version, then check the assembled pieces against your agreed inspection scope. Describe concerns with close-up photographs and the part name. A workshop image helps explain the operation; the acceptance criteria for your product should be written into the project specification and inspection plan.

03 · UV printing and artwork review

The photo shows finished tiles printed with space-theme artwork. Direct printing on tiles and printed retail packaging are different project items: identify which surface needs your artwork. Send the artwork version and your intended colors, then review placement and appearance on the agreed sample. For the files and packaging information to prepare, use the packaging and artwork guide.

04 · Dimension checks

The factory material includes a close-up of a tile being measured with a gauge. For a buyer, the next step is to request the relevant dimensions for the chosen model and agree which measurements belong in the inspection record. Keep MINI, standard tiles and other connection formats distinct in your brief. For a combined build, review the actual connecting components together and record the result with the sample reference.

05 · Weight checks

The electronic-scale photo shows bagged components being weighed. Use the approved component list as the starting point for your own sample check: record each part type and quantity, the bag arrangement and the complete set reference. Ask which weight checks apply to the selected configuration. Agree any inspection limits and recording method with the project team, so your buyer, inspector and packing team work from the same version.

06 · Inner packing and outer cartons

Our packing photos show components going into inner boxes and plain inner boxes arranged inside an outer carton. Confirm the inner layout alongside your retail artwork: where the components, instructions and any separate accessories belong. For your quotation, request the packing information for the chosen version, including sets per carton and carton measurements where needed. Keep the approved layout and artwork revision together in the order record.

07 · Shipment and container loading

Shipment material records pallet preparation and container loading. Plan the arrangements for your order around its actual destination, carton information and agreed shipping terms. Include any inspection or packing-photo requests in the brief, and confirm the scope before production and shipment. Ask for order-specific loading and dispatch information when reviewing the shipment plan. The packing method and transport arrangements should match the confirmed order.

How to evaluate a magnetic tiles factory

Use the process photographs above as a starting point. A photograph explains an operation; your project needs a product reference, an agreed inspection scope and a record of what was reviewed.

1. Match the factory evidence to your product

Ask which molding, assembly, printing and packing stages apply to the selected SKU. Request dated process references for that version and identify any steps handled separately. Keep the component list and artwork revision beside the photographs; equipment counts alone do not establish output for your order.

2. Check the document scope

Ask for the complete document and model annexes, then compare the model code, sample photographs, components and packaging version with your brief. Use the safety testing document guide and report coverage review to record unresolved differences. A report for another model is not evidence for this configuration. Documents are matched to SKU and destination per project.

3. Review the physical sample

Record the SKU, version, component count and sample photographs. Screen for cracks, exposed magnets, inconsistent holding and, where applicable, track joints and car movement using the 10-minute sample inspection checklist and printable A4 sheet. This first-look screen does not establish safety compliance or replace the agreed inspection plan. Keep observations and requested changes with the sample reference.

4. Keep approval records together

Ask which sample, component list, artwork revision and packing layout will be used for production. Record open questions before approval. For colors, set configuration and packaging choices, continue to the OEM and private label project page; use the manufacturer overview to compare tile-based product directions.

Turn the factory review into a clear brief

Send the SKU, version, quantity per SKU, target market, packaging direction and sample requirements. Add the checks you want to discuss and any new component or tooling needs. Review report verification and coverage. Share the selected product and destination with that request. The MOQ and samples guide helps you organize the next review.
