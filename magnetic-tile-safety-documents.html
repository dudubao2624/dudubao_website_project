Magnetic tile safety documents: what to prepare before your first order

Magnetic tile safety documents are easier to review when every file belongs to a defined product version and has a responsible person. Before your first order, prepare a document index that connects the sample, component list, assessment evidence, destination paperwork and final artwork. The aim is a clear decision at each project stage, with unresolved questions visible before the next approval.

Magnetic tile safety documents start with a controlled product brief

Set up one folder for each proposed SKU and configuration revision, with a document index at the front. Record the destination, intended age group, brand, component list, sample reference and expected launch date. Mark each document as draft, under review or approved for its stated purpose.

This is a proposed buyer workflow. The legal document package depends on the product and destination. For a market overview, use the existing safety testing guide; for the EU framework, read EN 71 for magnetic tiles: what importers should check. Keep this page beside them to manage who supplies each item and when it is reviewed.

Use a consistent file reference such as SKU, revision, document type and date. Preserve the reviewed version when a replacement arrives, and show which version is current. An unexplained attachment called “final” should remain an open item.

For EU toys under the directive framework, the technical documentation provisions in Article 21 and Annex IV are the legal reference. The Commission's technical documentation guide explains the supporting records: relevant design, manufacturing, component and material information; chemical safety data sheets where relevant; manufacturing and storage locations; and notified-body records where that route applies. The working index above helps locate records; it does not replace the technical file.

Who prepares and checks the magnetic tile safety documents?

The buyer defines the intended product and supplies the brand, destination, proposed artwork and retailer requirements. Nominate one document lead so a packaging designer, sourcing colleague and reviewer work from the same revision.

The responsible manufacturer manages the product assessment and technical documentation. The importer checks the destination obligations and the manufacturer's supporting file. The Commission's EU market-placement guidance describes the distinct manufacturer, importer and distributor steps.

The laboratory or technical reviewer clarifies methods, applicable scope and evidence gaps. It cannot choose your commercial configuration or keep your artwork versions aligned. Agree who signs off technical questions and who records the resulting changes in the project file.

For a private-label project, settle the legal manufacturer role early. A brand name on the box may bring responsibilities beyond artwork approval. Write down the legal entity, contact and document duties before treating the role allocation as complete.

When to check: use four project checkpoints

1. Before sample selection

Choose the configuration and purpose of the sample review. Compare the D01 product configuration or A01-T ball-run configuration with your proposed component list. List modifications separately. If materials, accessories or artwork are still undecided, keep them marked as open rather than silently adopting a catalogue version.

2. Before order confirmation

Review the sample-to-document mapping and list every gap. Agree the assessment scope, responsible party and completion point for each action. Keep open document questions in the same project record. Keep testing and document review in the project schedule, with their timing confirmed separately.

3. Before production release and printing

Freeze the reference sample, configuration and artwork revision for their agreed purposes. Confirm that any technical changes have been reviewed. Keep dated approval records for the box, labels and instructions; the packaging and artwork guide helps organise the artwork inputs. Reopen the relevant check if the approved content changes.

4. Before dispatch and market placement

Compare the production reference with the reviewed specification and final document index. Record any differences, unresolved actions and the person deciding release. Dispatch review and legal market-placement review are separate checkpoints. Before market placement, the responsible party must verify that the applicable documents and obligations are complete.

Keep a simple evidence-to-product record

Use one line per supporting document: file reference, issuer, sample code, linked SKU revision, scope, differences, reviewer and decision date. Add the production or batch reference when available. A blank field stays open until evidence or a documented explanation fills it.

For configuration identity, D01-C has 102 pieces and A01-T has 119 pieces. Counts include the listed accessories; manuals are listed separately. These verified counts help identify the selected configuration, but matching a total does not demonstrate document coverage.

If the planned set changes colour, accessory mix, material or construction, log the change beside the affected evidence. The responsible reviewer should decide whether further assessment or testing is needed. Keep that decision with the revision, so the next person can understand why the evidence was retained or replaced.

Common file-preparation mistakes

  • Collecting covers without scope: keep complete evidence, including photographs, annexes and amendments, together.
  • Using one generic material label: identify the material for each relevant component and leave uncertain entries open.
  • Finishing artwork before legal details: resolve business identities, warnings and language needs before printing.
  • Leaving gaps with no owner: give every unresolved item a person, evidence needed, due date and review decision.
  • Replacing documents without a revision record: preserve the previous reviewed version and explain what changed.

Close the review with a decision: continue preparation, hold the affected approval, or escalate a contradiction. A tidy folder supports the decision; the contents and product connection determine whether the review is complete.

Importer questions

When should I start preparing magnetic tile safety documents?

Start before selecting the reference sample. Define the SKU, configuration, destination and responsible parties first. Review evidence gaps before order confirmation, artwork before printing, and the final production and document references before dispatch and market placement.

Can the first review use draft artwork and documents?

Drafts can help identify gaps during project preparation. Mark their status and record who will complete each item. Replace them with the required final documents and approved artwork at the agreed checkpoints; a draft cannot stand in for a required signed declaration.

Should every SKU have a separate document index?

A separate index for each configuration helps prevent revision mix-ups. Supporting evidence may be shared only where the product connection and applicable scope are established. Keep the basis for shared evidence visible and record differences for each SKU.

What should an unresolved-action entry contain?

Record the affected SKU revision, missing evidence or discrepancy, responsible person, due date and next decision. State which approval remains on hold. Close the entry only when the reviewer records the evidence or explanation that resolves it.

Bring a clear first-order document brief

Include your SKU, destination, component-list revision, proposed brand, packaging languages and open document questions in the existing inquiry form. For sample planning, allow about 7 days for in-stock samples or about 15 days for custom colour or packaging samples. These are preparation times, excluding transit. Custom and private-label orders start at 500 sets per SKU. Production takes 30–40 days after the sample and order details are confirmed. Review the MOQ and samples guide when preparing your project schedule.
