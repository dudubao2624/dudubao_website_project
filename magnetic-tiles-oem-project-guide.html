Magnetic Tiles OEM Manufacturer: How to Plan an OEM Project

Working with a magnetic tiles OEM manufacturer starts with a product decision: which existing set is closest to the range you want to launch? Choose that reference first, then define colors, packaging and sample approval. This guide helps brand buyers turn an idea into five practical stages, with a clear decision at each handover. What to decide with a magnetic tiles OEM manufacturer

Write a one-page brief before choosing artwork. Include the intended play experience, reference model, quantity for each version, target market, packaging direction and launch timing. Separate decisions already made from options you want reviewed. This lets the discussion address a defined set rather than an open collection of ideas.

Use an existing configuration as the starting reference. List proposed differences beside it: a color change, branded box, translated booklet or revised component mix. If your concept requires a new shape or structure, flag that separately for development review. The sample and production timing below should not be treated as a schedule for new tooling.

Step 1: choose the base set before designing the range

Choose the play activity you want the box to communicate. For a tile-building direction, compare D01-C, with 102 pieces, and D10-C, with 219 pieces. For a tube-run direction, review A01-T, with 119 pieces. These are reference configurations; confirm the selected sample and corresponding packaging for your project.

Compare the contents as well as the total. Piece counts include the listed accessories, while the booklet is listed separately. A larger total does not explain the quantity of each shape or the build shown in a photograph. Use the set configuration guide to list the components behind your proposed product image.

For example, if you are considering two palettes for the same base set, give each proposed version a separate line. Show the shared contents, the colors that differ and the intended packaging for each. Ask how those versions will be identified during sampling and how the MOQ applies to the proposal. Keeping those questions together makes the assortment easier to review without assuming that a shared base model means every detail can be combined.

Decision to close: name one reference SKU and version, attach its component list, and mark every proposed addition or removal. Keep an alternative model separate so its contents cannot accidentally enter the main brief.

Step 2: define color and packaging changes together

Distinguish product colors from printed colors. A brand color on a box is a separate approval from a color on a tile. Identify which components change, which remain as selected, and what physical or artwork reference the team should review. Describe any proposed tile printing by part, side and position, subject to project review.

For packaging, choose a direction before final layout. Your designer needs the project-confirmed dieline, the selected contents and the instruction language list. Use the private-label buyer's checklist to prepare the logo, barcode and warning-location brief. Keep the box illustration consistent with the configuration: a display build should not quietly introduce pieces outside the agreed set.

Decision to close: approve a written change list for sampling. For example, retaining the selected components while changing the palette and box artwork still requires those changed elements to be reviewed; an existing-set photo cannot approve them.

Step 3: choose the right sample review

State what each sample must answer. An in-stock sample can help you select a configuration. A custom color or packaging sample addresses a changed version. Ask which elements the proposed sample will represent and which remain open, then agree the review scope and schedule for that project.

In-stock samples take about 7 days; custom color or packaging samples take about 15 days. These are preparation times, excluding transit. Sample arrangements are confirmed per project. Allow a separate review period for your team to receive the sample, examine it and consolidate comments; do not treat preparation time as the entire approval cycle.

Compare the contents, colors, artwork and instructions against the brief. Use the sample inspection checklist to organize initial observations. Record any proposed correction with a photo, part name and sample reference, then agree whether it needs a revised sample. A visual review does not establish the documentation scope for the finished set.

Step 4: confirm one product and order specification

Bring the selected sample, component list and final artwork together before confirming the order details. Name the approved color references, box and booklet revisions, quantity per version and any open review items. Give one person responsibility for collecting your team's decision so a designer's artwork approval is not confused with approval of the entire product.

Custom and private-label orders start at 500 sets per SKU, subject to project confirmation. List every model and colorway with its proposed quantity. A combined assortment total does not automatically meet the requirement for each SKU; confirm the scope for the proposed versions. The MOQ and samples guide explains how to prepare that discussion.

Decision to close: confirm sample and order details against the same specification. If a part, color or artwork changes after review, identify the affected approval before treating the order as ready for production.

Step 5: plan production from the confirmation point

Production takes 30–40 days after the sample and order details are confirmed. Build your plan around that starting condition. An inquiry date, first artwork draft or initial sample selection is not the confirmation point. Agree the project schedule once the product and order details are settled.

Include the intended market and documentation needs in the brief early. We have EN 71 reports for selected samples; coverage of the current sets and target-market documentation is confirmed per project. Keep this review tied to the selected configuration and packaging. Testing requirements or product changes may affect the project schedule.

Put your own decisions on the schedule too. Name who checks the sample, who approves translated copy and who confirms the final order specification. Set internal review dates and consolidate feedback before sending a revision. If the team changes direction, identify which milestone needs another review and confirm the updated sequence. This gives the launch plan a practical basis without assuming that every approval will happen immediately.

Common planning pitfalls and how to avoid them

  • Designing the box before selecting the set. Choose the configuration first, then check the illustration, contents list and packaging layout against it.
  • Combining several versions into one quantity. Give each SKU and colorway its own line and confirm how the MOQ applies to the proposal.
  • Approving from an unnamed photograph. Identify the sample and artwork revision so everyone can find the exact reference behind the decision.
  • Starting the production clock during sampling. Place the confirmation milestone before the 30–40-day production period in your plan.
  • Changing a component after artwork approval. Recheck the contents, images, instructions and documentation scope affected by the change before release.

The buyer's decision checklist before production

Use these questions for the final handover. An unanswered item should have a named owner and a clear next step.

  • Is the selected SKU, version and component list unambiguous?
  • Are colors, proposed printing and packaging tied to approved references?
  • Do the box image and instructions match the selected contents?
  • Are quantities recorded for each SKU and colorway?
  • Have sample comments and the relevant documentation review been resolved?
  • Are the sample, order details and production starting point confirmed?
  • Are inspection arrangements and any remaining responsibilities agreed?

Buyer questions

Can I start with packaging changes only?

You can describe a project that retains an existing configuration and changes the branding or packaging. Identify the reference set and proposed files, then confirm feasibility, sample scope and approval requirements for the selected project.

Does a 500-set total cover several models?

Custom and private-label orders start at 500 sets per SKU, subject to project confirmation. List the quantity for each model and colorway; a combined total does not automatically qualify every proposed version.

When does the production period begin?

Production takes 30–40 days after the sample and order details are confirmed. Sample preparation and your review occur before that starting point. Confirm the project schedule when the specification is settled.
