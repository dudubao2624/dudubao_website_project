Custom magnetic tile sets: plan the mix, the count and the carton

A set's shape mix decides what children can build, its counting rule decides what the box can claim, and its packaging decides how many sets fit in a carton. Plan all three before you approve a sample.

Explore OEM optionsView the full product range

1. Start from what the set should build

Pick two or three target builds, such as a house, a vehicle or a tall tower. The shapes those builds need become your core list; anything extra can go into an add-on pack.

2. Write one counting rule

Decide whether the count includes wheels, cards, bases, balls or bags, and use that rule on the box, the listing and the packing list. Colourways can differ: our D10-C has 219 pieces and D10-M 226 in the same box size.

Common problem: box artwork that does not match the set, such as a wrong piece count or missing colours. What we do now: we check box proofs against the physical set before printing and mark colours with Pantone references.

3. List every shape and colour

Use one line per shape and colour with its quantity. Keep MINI tiles on a separate list, because they are a different size from standard tiles. Approve colours on a physical sample: a digital proof confirms layout and text, not colour.

4. Plan the carton early

The box size sets the carton count, and the carton count drives freight per set. As reference data, our D01 sets pack 5 to a 395 × 337 × 255 mm carton (17.25 kg gross) and D10 sets 2 to a 525 × 345 × 230 mm carton (17 kg); we confirm the figures for each order.

Common problem: adding an inner tray changes the box size, and with it the carton and the freight. What we do now: we confirm carton data before the mix is locked.

5. Count sets and packaging separately

Finished sets, printed boxes and printed tile designs each have their own minimum. A small run of sets can still need a full run of printed boxes. OEM and private-label production generally starts at 500 sets per SKU; printed tiles are counted per design.

What to put in your brief

  • Reference model and target builds
  • Counting rule
  • Quantity per shape and colour
  • Colour references
  • Box size and carton data
  • Target market and quantity per SKU
