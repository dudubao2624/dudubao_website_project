Buying guide for retailers and wholesale buyers

Wholesale Magnetic Tiles: How Retailers Choose Sets and Plan First Orders

Choosing wholesale magnetic tiles starts with a clear job for each set on your shelf. Retailers need to connect the play direction, component mix and color box with a workable first-order plan. This guide takes you from a short assortment brief to defined order lines, with decisions to make before samples and artwork are approved.

Start wholesale magnetic tiles selection with a role for each set

Write a one-line purpose for every proposed SKU: a core construction set, a larger building option, or a separate route-building theme. This gives your buying team a reason to retain a model. If two choices have the same role, compare their contents and presentation before adding both to the first order.

Keep the initial comparison manageable. Record model, colorway, total contents, packaging revision and the build you want to demonstrate. Use the wholesale range overview to distinguish existing branded stock from a private-label project. A retail assortment plan should identify the route for each line rather than combining them under one general quantity.

For a shop demonstration, list the components used in the displayed build. Check whether they come from one box or several, and make the caption match. An ambitious display is useful only when staff can explain what the buyer receives in the selected set.

Compare piece-count bands through the actual contents

Use piece-count bands to organize a shortlist, then examine the component mix within each band. The totals below include listed non-tile accessories; instruction books are listed separately. They describe different configurations, so a larger total alone does not establish the right assortment role.

Make a simple comparison sheet with columns for core shapes, route parts and accessories. For each proposed display, mark the required quantities. This exposes a missing component more clearly than comparing the headline totals. Keep different play systems as separate buying decisions until the selected components have been reviewed together.

For example, the D01-C contents list includes 35 small squares and 4 large squares; D10-C lists 42 small squares and 14 large squares. If your display depends on large square sections, record that demand explicitly. The total set counts do not scale each shape in the same proportion. Build the proposed display with the selected sample and retain a component tally with its photograph, so a later artwork or demonstration change can be checked against the same evidence.

Review the color box as part of the product

Prepare a box brief before artwork begins. Specify the model code, confirmed piece count, language versions, contents illustration and intended shelf orientation. Check the front, side and back as a connected set of information. A correct front panel cannot resolve a different configuration shown on the back.

Use the packaging and artwork guide to organize the dieline, editable files and revision names. Compare the proposed package with the sample contents. If a pictured structure needs extra sets, explain that clearly. Keep the barcode review tied to the selected SKU and box revision rather than copying an identifier from a similar pack.

For shelf planning, product records list a D01-C color box of 322 × 240 × 73 mm and a D10-C color box of 510 × 330 × 100 mm. These are recorded packaging references, not measurements of the current batch. Confirm the selected package before fixing shelf layouts or approving artwork. Review how staff will open, show and repack the sample.

Keep a compact approval pack containing the contents list, box panels, instructions and a photograph of the reviewed build. Give each file a revision name. When one file changes, check the other three for a matching update. This makes it easier to catch an older piece count, an extra accessory in an illustration or a language file referring to a different model before the order details are confirmed.

Build first-order lines around the MOQ

Custom and private-label orders start at 500 sets per SKU. For DUDUBAO-branded stock, small orders are supported subject to actual inventory. These are separate purchasing routes; identify which one applies before turning an assortment shortlist into order lines.

Give each line its own model, version, colorway, quantity and packaging reference. Mixed-SKU requests are reviewed separately; 500 sets in total does not automatically qualify for mixed-SKU production. Ask how the proposed colors and packaging affect the confirmed MOQ. Keep the answer with the same brief revision.

For example, a shortlist with a core building set and a route-building theme needs two defined lines, each with a quantity and sample purpose. First decide whether both have a clear retail role. Then review feasibility for each line. Avoid treating the assortment total as approval of every model and packaging combination.

Use a sample sequence with a decision at each step

  1. Select the existing direction. Name the SKU and the build you want to examine. Record the contents and photographs under one sample reference.
  2. Review proposed changes. List colors, box artwork, instructions and any configuration changes separately. Identify which items need a changed sample.
  3. Close the review. Mark each observation as approved, requiring revision or still open. Link the final sample reference to the order brief.
  4. Confirm the production starting point. Review the sample and order details together before agreeing the project schedule.

In-stock samples take about 7 days; custom color or packaging samples take about 15 days. These are preparation times, excluding transit. Production takes 30–40 days after the sample and order details are confirmed. Sample scope and schedule are confirmed per project; changes can affect timing. The MOQ and sample guide explains how to keep selection, sample review and order confirmation connected.

Common first-order pitfalls to catch early

  • Choosing by the largest number. Check whether the specific shapes and accessories support the product role you wrote down.
  • Approving a box before the contents. Compare artwork, instructions and component list under one revision.
  • Using one sample to approve several variants. State which model, colors and packaging that sample represents.
  • Leaving store demonstrations undefined. Record whether the display uses one set or multiple sets and prepare matching staff notes.
  • Moving documentation wording between models. Keep the selected SKU, configuration and intended market together in the project record.

We have EN 71 reports for selected samples; coverage of the current sets and target-market documentation is confirmed per project.

A decision checklist before sending your retail brief

Use this final check with the person reviewing the assortment and the person reviewing artwork. Any unresolved item should remain visible in the brief, with a named next step.

  • Every selected SKU has a distinct retail role and a quantity per version.
  • The piece count and component list match the proposed demonstration.
  • The buying route and MOQ have been reviewed for each order line.
  • The color box, language files and barcode review refer to the selected version.
  • Each sample has a purpose, reference and written review result.
  • The intended market, documentation questions and required timing are included.

Buyer FAQ

What should a retailer compare before choosing wholesale magnetic tiles?

Compare the role of each set, its component list, the proposed demonstration and the color box. Use piece-count bands to organize the shortlist, then check the exact contents and packaging revision with the sample.

Does an assortment total of 500 sets meet the private-label MOQ?

Custom and private-label orders start at 500 sets per SKU. Mixed-SKU requests are reviewed separately; 500 sets in total does not automatically qualify for mixed-SKU production. List a quantity for each model, version and colorway.

How should the first sample review be scheduled?

In-stock samples take about 7 days; custom color or packaging samples take about 15 days. These are preparation times, excluding transit. Production takes 30–40 days after the sample and order details are confirmed. Confirm the sample scope and project schedule.

Prepare your first retail assortment brief

List the selected SKUs, quantity per version, buying route, target market, color-box brief and sample purpose. Include your required timing and open documentation questions so the next review can address the actual assortment.
