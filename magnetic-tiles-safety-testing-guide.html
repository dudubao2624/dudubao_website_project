Magnetic Tiles Safety Testing: Documents Buyers Should Request

A brand buying magnetic tiles needs a document package that identifies the proposed product, explains the applicable market route and connects the evidence to production. A report cover with a familiar standard name is only the start. Begin with the SKU, configuration, materials, intended age group and destination, then check what each document actually covers.

Build a request around one product version

Ask for the complete report, including sample photographs, model lists, results, amendments and annexes. Pair it with the component list, material and colour references, packaging artwork and instructions for the proposed SKU. Record the report number and the product revision together. If an internal item code differs from your SKU, request a documented cross-reference rather than relying on a similar product name.

Keep a separate line for each destination. The following is a buyer document checklist; the applicable assessment and any additional requirements depend on the actual product and market.

Choose the destination document route

European Union: EN71 evidence and the CE file

Request the safety assessment, technical documentation, signed EC Declaration of Conformity and proposed CE marking, warnings and traceability information. A laboratory report supports this file; a CE logo alone does not identify its contents. See the European Commission's placing-toys guidance.

For EN71 reports, identify EN 71-1 for mechanical and physical properties, EN 71-2 for flammability and EN 71-3 for migration of certain elements. Check the edition, amendments, scope and any restrictions against the official harmonised standards references and the consolidated decision. Do not treat three standard names as the whole assessment for every toy.

Regulation (EU) 2025/2509 entered into force on 1 January 2026; its main product requirements apply from 1 August 2030. Plan future document changes, including the digital product passport, alongside today's review rather than treating them as already required for every current order. Source: European Commission toy safety overview.

Great Britain: identify CE or UKCA before requesting files

Great Britain currently recognises the CE route for toys alongside UKCA. Ask which route is intended and request the corresponding declaration of conformity, technical evidence, marking and English warnings. A CE route uses an EU declaration; a UKCA route uses a UK declaration. Check the relevant assessment procedure rather than assuming a second complete test package is always needed. Northern Ireland has a separate route and should be reviewed separately. Source: GOV.UK toy safety guidance.

United States: ASTM F963, 16 CFR part 1250 and the CPC

Request an applicability review for ASTM F963 as incorporated by 16 CFR part 1250, together with evidence for other applicable children's product rules, such as lead and phthalates. Check the Commission-accepted standard version and which sections apply to the proposed toy. Source: CPSC toy safety guidance.

For children's products subject to testing and certification requirements, request the Children's Product Certificate (CPC) and its supporting CPSC-accepted laboratory reports, taking account of applicable exceptions. For an overseas-made product, identify the US importer responsible for the CPC. Ask the importer to check current certificate eFiling requirements as well. Source: CPSC CPC guidance. Keep production and batch references with the required tracking information.

Australia and New Zealand: review each country separately

Where an AS/NZS ISO 8124 report is proposed, identify its parts and editions and ask how they support the destination's mandatory requirements. In Australia, check the toys containing magnets standard and, where applicable, the lead and other elements standard. Their legal references can differ from the newest published standard. A newer report date or edition alone does not settle the comparison.

For New Zealand, review the children's toy standard and its age scope and the scope of the small high-powered magnets ban. Do not carry an Australian conclusion across without that review. Where flammability is part of the requested evidence, identify the applicable AS/NZS ISO 8124.2 scope; ISO's Part 2 overview explains the subject without establishing a local legal route.

Ask what was examined, not just which standard is on the cover

Use the following questions to read the relevant mechanical and chemical results. The laboratory should identify applicable methods and exclusions for the selected toy; these questions are not instructions for destructive testing at home.

  • Magnets: which magnet or magnetic component was assessed, what small-parts assessment and magnetic flux index result are recorded where applicable, and what happened after the specified use-and-abuse sequence? A statement about strong attraction is not the same measurement. CPSC's toy guidance separately addresses magnets released during testing and flux index.
  • Small parts, balls and edges: which as-received or released parts were examined, what intended age group was used, and which findings or warnings apply? Check balls and accessories as well as the tile shell.
  • Mechanical integrity: where applicable, locate the tension, torque, drop, impact and compression methods, the tested shapes and the resulting observations on cracks, seams, accessible magnets and sharp points. Ask why any item was excluded rather than treating a blank result as evidence.
  • Materials and coatings: which colours, plastics, paints, inks, metals and other accessible materials were sampled for element migration or other applicable chemical requirements? A material declaration alone does not identify the tested sample.
  • Added functions: ask whether sound-making parts, powered accessories or batteries change the assessment scope. Keep those parts visible in the component list instead of transferring a plain-tile result to a different set.

For mechanical and magnet topics, see CPSC's section-by-section toy guidance. For the EN71 part titles and electrical toy reference, see the official EU standards decision.

Match the report to the SKU and the proposed batch

Use one review record with the following fields. This mapping is a buyer review method, not a statement that every order needs a new laboratory test.

  • Product identity: exact SKU, version, component quantities, intended age group and photographs of the sample, box and instructions.
  • Report identity: laboratory, report number, dates, complete page count, amendments and model annexes. Check the issuer's published verification method and, for relevant US testing, laboratory acceptance for the applicable scope.
  • Scope: tested parts, material and colour references, standard editions, methods, results, exclusions and the basis for any grouped models.
  • Production link: order or batch reference, manufacturing date and the specification revision linking production to the tested sample. If the report predates the batch, record how that connection is established.
  • Change review: differences in magnets, plastics, pigments, joining process, component mix, suppliers, artwork or instructions, with the responsible party's decision on whether further assessment or testing is needed.
  • Market file: declaration or CPC where applicable, required labels, importer details, instructions and any retailer-specific requests. Keep open points and the person responsible for resolving them visible.

A model name in an annex does not by itself show that the current materials, complete set and batch were examined. Equally, a report's age alone does not decide its usefulness: examine changes to the product, the applicable rules and the supporting production records together.

Buyer FAQ

Which EN71 reports should a magnetic tile buyer request?

Request an applicability review covering EN 71-1 mechanical and physical properties, EN 71-2 flammability and EN 71-3 element migration, with the relevant editions, tested components and any exclusions. These report titles alone do not replace the full market document file.

Does Great Britain always require a separate UKCA test package?

No. Great Britain currently recognises the CE route alongside UKCA. Identify the intended route and its declaration and technical evidence; review Northern Ireland separately.

Can one report cover every SKU and batch?

Coverage needs a documented connection between the tested sample and the selected SKU, materials, configuration and production reference. A related model name alone does not establish that connection; changes may need further review or testing.

What should a magnetic flux index result be matched to?

Match it to the identified magnet or magnetic component, the applicable method and the relevant small-parts and use-and-abuse assessment. A general statement about attraction strength does not identify that evidence.
