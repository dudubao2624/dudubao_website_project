How to compare a magnetic tiles supplier in China

Write the same starting specification for every factory under consideration. Include the intended product, reference configuration, proposed changes, quantity per version, target market, packaging direction and required timing. Mark undecided items clearly. Otherwise, one reply may describe an existing set while another describes a changed palette and branded box.

Keep alternative products on separate lines. At DUDUBAO, D01-C has 102 pieces and D10-C has 219 pieces. For route-based alternatives, A01-T has 119 pieces and A03-T has 73 pieces. These totals include listed accessories; manuals are listed separately. Confirm the selected sample and corresponding packaging. Use the full component list when defining an equivalent comparison.

Compare five areas with the same evidence standard

Create one column per supplier and use the five rows below. For each answer, save the file or sample reference, date, observation and next action. Mark the result as matched, needs clarification or needs revision. Leave missing evidence open rather than turning it into a positive or negative assumption about the factory.

Set your priorities before reviewing replies. A packaging-led launch may need more attention to artwork coordination; an expanded configuration may need closer component control. Record why a criterion matters to your project. Avoid an overall score that conceals an unresolved requirement.

Step 1: compare samples with a defined purpose

State whether each sample is for selecting an existing configuration, reviewing a color change or checking packaging. Agree which elements it will represent before comparing the results. An existing-set sample and a proposed custom version answer different questions; label their status in the comparison sheet.

Use the same review setup and record the same observations for each relevant sample. Count the contents, identify the pieces examined and photograph any concern beside its component name. For a build or route, retain the instructions and the exact arrangement used. The sample inspection checklist provides a first-look recording method.

Separate observation from conclusion. A note such as “the illustrated build needs two additional squares” identifies a question that can be resolved. A general label such as “poor sample” leaves the next reviewer without a useful action. Compare the response to the specific issue and retain the revised reference if the sample changes.

Step 2: check how configuration records handle changes

Ask for a list that identifies each component and its quantity, with colors recorded where relevant. Keep the complete-set count, accessory list and booklet reference together. A headline total cannot explain whether two proposals include the same shapes or how a changed version differs from the original.

Use one proposed change to examine the revision process. For example, ask how a new palette would be recorded while retaining the selected quantities. Look for a clear difference list, a current revision and an explanation of which images or instructions need updating. This is a practical review exercise, rather than a demand to redesign the set before selection.

Keep component descriptions precise. In C01-C, the 99-piece track set, the vehicle is an LED track car. Its track pieces and LED track car are ABS. Record other component materials separately. Do not let a general material field or a photograph from another version settle the specification.

Step 3: review packaging capability through a concrete proposal

Describe the packaging you need, then ask which format, dieline, inner layout and artwork files should be reviewed for that product. Identify what a proposed packaging sample will demonstrate. A useful answer connects the selected contents with the box, booklet and any inserts rather than showing unrelated packaging examples.

Check the handover as well as the appearance. Who supplies the artwork? Which template revision will the designer use? How will comments on copy, images and panel orientation be consolidated? Use the packaging and artwork guide to prepare those questions. Confirm dimensions and packing details for the selected version before using them in your plan.

Check specific features against the model reference: D10-C has no carry handle. If a presentation image shows a different packaging feature, identify that difference before it enters the brief. Keep proposed options separate from the product you have actually selected.

Step 4: compare document scope and unresolved actions

Keep the selected SKU, component-list revision, sample identity, packaging and target market beside the supporting document references. Ask the project team to explain the connection and identify any differences. A similar model name should lead to a clarification rather than an assumed match.

We have EN 71 reports for selected samples; coverage of the current sets and target-market documentation is confirmed per project.

For the comparison, record what is established, what remains open, who will resolve it and when the next review occurs. Use the first-order document checklist to organize the file. Keep document review separate from your observations of sample appearance.

Step 5: agree a communication rhythm you can follow

Propose written checkpoints for brief confirmation, sample readiness, consolidated feedback and the revised decision. Agree a contact responsible for gathering answers on each side. This gives both teams a workable rhythm without assuming an unconfirmed response-time commitment.

Review the usefulness of replies: do they answer each numbered question, identify the current attachment and distinguish confirmed details from open points? When something changes, does the reply explain which earlier decision it replaces? Keep the answer with the comparison record so your team can assess clarity across the whole review.

Include timing on the same basis. DUDUBAO in-stock samples take about 7 days; custom color or packaging samples take about 15 days. These are preparation times, excluding transit, with arrangements confirmed per project. Production takes 30–40 days after the sample and order details are confirmed. Allow time for your own review between those stages.

A decision checklist before selecting the supplier

Use the following sequence at your final shortlist review. Choose whether to continue, seek a defined revision or hold the affected decision. Record a reason and next action for every open item.

  1. Confirm that every proposal answers the same product brief.
  2. Identify the sample and the purpose it actually demonstrates.
  3. Match the contents and proposed changes to a current configuration record.
  4. Connect the packaging proposal to that product and its artwork revision.
  5. Assign unresolved document questions and review dates.
  6. Agree communication checkpoints and the next approval record.
  7. Record quantity per SKU and the conditions that start production.

Custom and private-label DUDUBAO orders start at 500 sets per SKU. List each proposed model and colorway with its quantity, and confirm the project scope. The MOQ and samples guide helps connect the selected version to the next sample decision.

Common mistakes in a factory comparison

  • Comparing different briefs. Return to one specification and mark every proposed difference.
  • Counting attractive images as sample approval. Identify the sample, contents and reviewed arrangement.
  • Accepting an unnamed final file. Retain a revision and approval purpose for each attachment.
  • Judging communication from speed alone. Check whether replies close questions and preserve decisions.
  • Letting a strong result hide an open requirement. Keep unresolved product, packaging or document items visible until reviewed.

Buyer questions

What should I compare first when choosing a magnetic tiles supplier in China?

Start with one shared product brief. Compare identified samples, configuration records, packaging proposals, supporting document scope and communication checkpoints against that same brief. Record differences before selecting a supplier.

Can I compare factories using the total piece count?

Use the total to identify a configuration, then compare component types, quantities, colors and accessories. A matching total alone does not establish equivalent contents. Keep manuals separate where the selected configuration lists them separately.

How should I handle an unanswered comparison item?

Mark it open, name the evidence or clarification needed, assign a responsible person and agree the next review point. Keep the affected selection decision conditional until the question is resolved.
