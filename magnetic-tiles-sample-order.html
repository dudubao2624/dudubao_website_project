Magnetic tiles sample order: what to expect

A magnetic tiles sample order should answer a specific buying question: which set fits your range, whether proposed colors work, or how your packaging presents the selected contents. Define that question before arranging the sample. This guide explains what to prepare, how the approximate preparation times fit the project, and how to record findings without losing the link between the product, box and instructions.

Prepare your magnetic tiles sample order brief

Start with the model code, version, intended market and the decision you need to make. Choose a reference from the D01-C building set, D10-C larger building set or the relevant route set. Give each proposed model its own line so the sample purpose stays clear.

Add quantity per intended order line, packaging direction, instruction languages and preferred review timing. Mark proposed color, artwork or component changes individually. Prepare one current brief for the team, with a named person to consolidate feedback. An undecided field can remain open with a question beside it.

  • Product reference: SKU, configuration, contents-list revision and proposed changes.
  • Sample purpose: the selection, color, packaging or instruction question to resolve.
  • Buyer inputs: target market, quantities, languages, artwork status and review contact.
  • Expected review: the build, route, packaging panel or detail you want to examine.

Agree what the sample will represent

An in-stock sample can help you examine an existing configuration. A custom color or packaging sample addresses the proposed changes agreed for your project. Ask which elements will be represented physically, which will be reviewed as files, and which remain open. Keep that scope beside the sample reference.

For example, a buyer keeping the selected contents while changing box artwork should identify both the existing product reference and the proposed packaging revision. Record whether the sample represents the box layout, printed appearance, product colors or only some of those items. A decision should name the element examined.

Custom and private-label orders start at 500 sets per SKU. This is the order MOQ; agree the sample scope separately. List quantities per model and version when preparing the project. Use the MOQ and samples guide for the wider order pathway.

Ask the project team to confirm the preparation starting point and planned readiness date once the sample scope and inputs are clear. Keep transit and your own review time as separate calendar entries. A sample prepared in about seven days has not thereby been promised to arrive or complete your review within seven days.

Reserve time for unpacking, comments, clarification and any agreed revision. Confirm revised timing if colors, packaging or configuration change during preparation. New shapes, structures and tooling need a separate development review; the approximate periods above should stay tied to the stated sample types.

Production takes 30–40 days after the sample and order details are confirmed. Keep that confirmation point visible after sampling and review. An initial inquiry or receipt of an existing sample does not establish that the final product and order details are confirmed.

Receive and review the sample in five steps

  1. Identify it before assembly. Photograph the box, model label and enclosed instructions. Record the date received, sample reference and any difference from the agreed scope.
  2. Count by component group. Lay out the parts against the selected list. Note what was counted and what remains unexamined. Keep accessories in the complete-set total and the manual separately.
  3. Review the intended activity. Follow the current instructions for the agreed build or route. Record the parts, arrangement and observed result. Use the 10-minute inspection checklist for the initial physical screen.
  4. Compare presentation. Review product colors, box panels, images and instructions against the named references. Use clear photo labels for each comment and keep the current artwork file beside the record.
  5. Consolidate the findings. Separate observations, requested changes and unanswered questions. Assign the next action and identify the approval affected before sharing one review record.

Adapt the activity review to the selected model. For A01-T, identify the supplied ball and reviewed tube route. For A03-T, add the bell positions. For C01-C, confirm the current LED track car setup and instructions before observing its operation.

A sample review record you can copy

Use one record per sample and revision. Start product, packaging and documentation status as Unconfirmed. Complete the fields below during review, then retain the photographs and referenced files together. The downloadable version provides a blank header and a repeatable finding entry.

Close product, packaging and document decisions separately

Give each finding a result: resolved with a named reference, awaiting clarification, or requiring a defined revision. If you approve an element, name its scope and evidence. “Product colors approved against sample S01” leaves box artwork and documentation with their own decisions. Keep a replaced reference in the history and identify the current one.

Review the packaging and artwork guide when a changed component or image affects the box or booklet. Reopen the affected fields after a revision. Agree whether another physical sample or file review is needed, and compare the response with the original finding before closing it.

Common mistakes in a sample order

  • Using preparation time as an arrival date. Put preparation, transit and buyer review in separate schedule entries.
  • Leaving custom changes unnamed. Identify the component, color reference or packaging revision that the sample must represent.
  • Approving with “looks good.” State the element, sample reference, evidence and remaining conditions.
  • Writing a vague defect comment. Add the affected part, expected reference, observed difference and photograph.
  • Closing every field after a successful build. Retain separate packaging, instruction and documentation decisions.

Your sample-order readiness checklist

  • The selected model, version and sample purpose are written down.
  • Proposed changes and required buyer files have named references.
  • The preparation starting point and readiness date are confirmed for the project.
  • Transit, internal review and potential revision have separate planning entries.
  • The reviewer has the contents list, current instructions and blank record.
  • Open findings have an owner, due date and a defined next decision.

Buyer questions

How long does a magnetic tiles sample order take to prepare?

In-stock samples take about 7 days; custom color or packaging samples take about 15 days. These are preparation times, excluding transit. Confirm the sample scope, starting point and schedule per project, and allow separately for buyer review.

Does an in-stock sample approve my custom packaging?

Record exactly what the sample represents. An existing product sample can support configuration selection; the proposed custom packaging needs its own identified review and decision. Agree which physical samples and files will be used for that review.

What should I record when the sample needs a change?

Record the SKU, sample reference, affected component or panel, expected reference, observed difference and photographs. Add the requested action, owner, due date and closure evidence, then identify the scope awaiting approval.

When does production timing begin?

Production takes 30–40 days after the sample and order details are confirmed. Sample preparation, transit, buyer review and any agreed revisions precede that confirmation point. Confirm the schedule for the selected project.
