STEM magnetic toys for retailers: organize by play

Start with four shopper questions: What can I build? How does the ball follow a route? Where does the ball meet a bell? How do I lay out a vehicle track? Give each question a place in the assortment. This creates a practical explanation for carrying several sets without relying on the piece total to distinguish them.

Use the same sequence in shelf cards and online category descriptions: build shapes, follow a ball, explore a bell route, plan a vehicle route. These are activity descriptions for range planning. Review intended ages, instructions and product statements for the selected configuration and target market before finalizing retail copy.

Give D01-C and D10-C different building jobs

Place these two choices within the same building layer, then explain the component difference. D01-C lists 35 small squares and four large squares. D10-C lists 42 small squares and 14 large squares. Use those quantities to plan the selected build rather than scaling a display in proportion to the total piece count.

Write a separate display brief for each model: required shapes, quantities, finished arrangement and a photograph from the reviewed sample. D10-C is a separate set choice; a shopper does not need to buy D01-C first. Keep the larger model when its shape mix and display give the range a clear second building option.

Explain the difference between ball routes and vehicle tracks

Use A01-T to frame the clear-tube route story and A03-T to frame the bell-route story. The two listed ball types differ: wooden balls for A01-T and iron balls for A03-T. Demonstrate each with its own selected contents. The tube and bell ball-run buying guide supports the detailed two-model comparison.

Give C01-C a separate vehicle-track label. Use the name LED track car consistently, and confirm its drive system, power source, supplied items and any required tools with the current sample and instructions. Keep route and setup details beside the display. A photograph of an earlier vehicle should not determine those instructions.

Shared shelf branding can connect these play stories. A combined physical build needs a review of the exact parts used together. If your display combines packs, identify them and show which pieces belong to each box. The play-system overview provides the wider product context.

Build the shelf plan in four steps

  1. Write the range story. Give each of the four play layers a short heading and each of the five models a one-sentence purpose. Have a colleague explain the choices using those sentences.
  2. Assign proposed facings. Sketch one facing for each selected model as an initial planning exercise. Add the intended display location and measure the space using the project-confirmed boxes. Adjust the sketch to the fixture.
  3. Prepare one card per model. Include the model code, complete-set count, play action and a reviewed one-pack image. Keep any extra display pieces identified in the caption.
  4. Walk the comparison. Ask staff to move from the core building choice to the larger choice, then explain the three route directions. Rewrite any label that leaves two models sounding interchangeable.

Check handling and repacking during that walk. D10-C has no carry handle. Review how the selected box will be lifted, opened and returned to the fixture. Confirm actual packaging before fixing shelf dimensions or photography; the packaging guide helps organize the artwork references.

Turn each shelf role into a sample demonstration

Ask for a sample with a defined decision: a building comparison, visible ball route, bell interaction or vehicle-track layout. Record the parts used, instruction step, setup and observed result. Keep one short demonstration per shelf card so staff can reproduce it and explain the contents without improvising a larger structure.

Keep material wording attached to its component. ABS is documented for the plastic bodies of D01-C and D10-C, the transparent tubes of A01-T and A03-T, and the track pieces and LED track car of C01-C. Confirm other component materials separately when needed for your specification.

Use the magnetic tiles sample order guide to prepare the review. Custom and private-label orders start at 500 sets per SKU. Record quantities for each proposed model and version; review the resulting order lines with the retailer first-order guide.

Common pitfalls in a five-model range

  • A shelf arranged only by totals. Put the play heading beside the count so a 73-piece bell set has a clear role alongside a larger building set.
  • Two building choices with identical explanations. Show the shape demand and reviewed display that distinguish D01-C from D10-C.
  • A combined display presented as one box. Count the packs used and identify additional components before approving imagery.
  • One setup note for every moving accessory. Keep the selected ball, route or LED track car instructions with the relevant model.
  • Product statements copied across the range. Match each statement to its SKU, components and project review.

A range decision checklist for the retail buyer

  • Each model has a named play layer and a distinct shelf purpose.
  • Both building choices have their own component-based display brief.
  • Ball, bell and vehicle routes use the selected model's contents and instructions.
  • Shelf space and handling have been checked with the selected packaging.
  • Every card and online listing identifies the complete-set count and reviewed images.
  • Sample decisions, quantities per SKU and open documentation questions are recorded.

Retain a model when the sample supports its proposed role and staff can explain that role clearly. Revise the display or shortlist when a question remains open. This gives the buying discussion a concrete assortment to review.

Buyer questions

How can five models form one retail range?

Use four play layers: shape building with D01-C and D10-C, clear-tube ball routes with A01-T, bell ball routes with A03-T, and vehicle tracks with C01-C. These are suggested shelf roles to review with the selected samples.

Does the shelf sequence mean shoppers must buy the sets in order?

Each listed model is a separate set choice. The sequence organizes the retail explanation. Any proposed combined build needs a review of the exact components and a caption identifying the packs used.

What should the sample brief include for a retail range?

List the selected models, quantity per SKU, target market, packaging direction and one demonstration question per model. Include shelf-space requirements, instruction languages and any product or documentation points still to confirm.
